**Stance: no foundation-level AI policy, and a provenance floor that predates the question.** The
other foundations in this bundle publish a position their projects inherit. OWASP publishes a
contributor-agreement requirement that never mentions AI:

> Projects **must** use OWASP's recommended contributor agreement,
> [DCO](https://github.com/apps/dco) to ensure that all contributions are the **original work of the
> contributor** and/or they have permission to contribute that code or change. Projects with an
> existing contributor agreement are exempt from this policy as long as the contributor agreement
> doesn't conflict with OWASP's license requirements, copyright assignment requirements, and **covers
> the risks of plagiarized code**.[^owasp-projects-policy]

Read that clause with the rest of this bundle in view and it is *almost* an AI policy. It asks for
original work. It names plagiarism risk as a thing an agreement must cover. Those are the two
concepts every provenance-based AI position here is built from.

**It was written on 2021-09-30 and has not been revisited since.** The policy file has changed twice
in 2025, both times for broken links. So this is not a foundation that considered generative AI and
declined to legislate; it is a floor poured before the question arrived, which happens to be made of
the right material.

## The absence is established, not merely unfound

`OWASP/www-policy` is the foundation's policy corpus — bylaws, code of conduct, committees, projects,
membership, and the rest. **Across all 60 markdown files there is not one occurrence** of
*AI*, *LLM*, *generative AI*, *artificial intelligence*, *machine-generated*, *Copilot* or *large
language model*, measured 2026-09-06.

Two controls make that number worth something. The same sweep matched *OWASP* in **56** of the 60
files, so it was reading them. And a copy of `operational/projects.md` with one sentence appended —
*"Contributions generated by generative AI must be disclosed"* — matched, so the checker can fail.

An earlier pass of this sweep returned **three** hits and all three were `insta`**`llm`**`ents`. A
bare `LLM` inside a word is the failure mode to watch for here, and the count above is word-bounded.

## What the projects did instead, which is the consequence worth recording

Three OWASP entities have reached three arrangements, none of them derived from a shared rule:

| | What it does about AI |
|---|---|
| [Dependency-Track](../projects/dependency-track.md) | permits it; forbids every trace in the commit — no assistant trailer, no session link, no generated-with footer — beneath the mandated DCO sign-off |
| [cdxgen](../projects/cdxgen.md) | asks nothing of contributors; publishes a machine-readable declaration of **its own** AI usage per source path |
| OWASP Developer Guide | requires declaration in the pull request or issue, with removal as the sanction |

The Developer Guide's rule is the closest thing to a foundation position and it explicitly is not
one — it scopes itself to the project that wrote it:

> If it is genuinely helpful to use generative AI then it must be declared in any pull request or
> issue, failure to do so can result in the contribution being closed or even deleted. … **AI slop
> drains maintainers' time and is actively harmful to this project.**[^owasp-devguide-contributing]

*This project*, not OWASP projects. Nothing in it reaches a sibling.

**So one foundation umbrella covers a project that forbids AI attribution outright and a project that
publishes its AI usage as a badge.** They are not in tension with any rule, because there is no rule
for them to be in tension with.

## Why this belongs in `foundations/` despite having no policy

The other three records here describe **floors that exist**. This one describes a floor that exists
and stops short, which is a different fact and a more common one.

It also matters for how a reader reasons about inheritance. A contributor to an OWASP project cannot
answer *"what does the foundation require of me about AI?"* by reading the foundation — the answer is
*nothing*, and the real answer is in the project. That is the opposite of the
[Linux Foundation](linux-foundation.md) and [ASF](apache-software-foundation.md) cases, where the
foundation text is the first thing to read and the project may narrow it.

**And the hook is sitting there unused.** [GCC](../projects/gcc.md) reached its position by extending
the copyright threshold it already applied; [Asahi Linux](../projects/asahi-linux.md) reached its by
extending an existing ban on leaked vendor material. Both took machinery they already ran and pointed
it at a new question. OWASP has the equivalent machinery — original work, plagiarism risk — in a
mandatory clause, and has not pointed it anywhere. **Whether that is deliberate restraint or simply
unexamined is not stated anywhere, and is not inferred here.**

## What a contributor must do

**Sign off your commits.** The DCO is mandatory across OWASP projects unless the project has its own
contributor agreement, and it certifies that what you submit is your original work or that you have
the right to submit it. Then **read the project**, because the foundation says nothing further about
AI and the projects disagree with each other: one wants no trace of a tool in your commit, another
asks nothing at all.

## Re-verification notes

The policy corpus is `github.com/OWASP/www-policy`, rendered at `owasp.org/www-policy/`. **Sweep the
repository rather than the website** — the rendered site gives no way to check for an absence across
the whole corpus, and an absence is the claim here.

Word-bound any `LLM` pattern. The three false positives in the first pass of this record were all the
same English word.

Two things would change this record. A foundation-level AI policy, most plausibly as a clause added
to the contributor-agreement paragraph, since that is where the concepts already live. Or the
Developer Guide's rule being widened past *"this project"*, which would make it the first OWASP text
with reach.

One thing was checked and left open. The projects policy mandates a contributor agreement, and
`cdxgen` publishes no `CONTRIBUTING.md`, no code of conduct, and ran no DCO check on a merged pull
request examined 2026-09-05. Whether it qualifies under the *"existing contributor agreement"*
exemption is **not established**, and no claim of non-compliance is made here.
