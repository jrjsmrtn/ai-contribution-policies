**Stance: permitted, at foundation level, as a default that projects may override.** The Linux
Foundation's *Policy Guidance Regarding Use of Generative AI Tools for Open Source Software
Development* is the most **upstream** document in this bundle: it is not one project's rule but a
baseline across LF projects.[^lf-generative-ai]

> Code or other content generated in whole or in part using AI tools can be contributed to Linux
> Foundation projects.[^lf-generative-ai]

The framing is deliberately deflationary:

> Open source software has thrived for decades based on the merits of each technical contribution
> that is openly contributed to and reviewed by community peers. **Development and review of code
> generated by AI tools should be treated no differently.**[^lf-generative-ai]

## It does not invoke the DCO — verified, not assumed

The page contains **zero occurrences** of *"Developer Certificate of Origin"*, *"DCO"* or
*"Signed-off-by"* (checked 2026-08-05 against the fetched page text, not inferred from
reading).[^lf-generative-ai]

That is the most consequential thing about this document, given the company it keeps.
[QEMU](../projects/qemu.md) reasons from the DCO to a prohibition; [Git](../projects/git.md)
reasons from it to a volume-scoped caution; the [Linux kernel](../projects/linux-kernel.md) reasons
from it to a certification-relocation rule. The foundation hosting many of those projects reasons
from **licence compatibility and third-party rights** instead, and arrives at permission.

Three projects and their foundation, one instrument, four different destinations. Any summary that
explains a stance by *"they require a DCO"* is explaining nothing — and this record is the reason to
stop using that shorthand.

## The two actual obligations

Both are about **rights in the output**, not about the tool:

1. **Tool terms must not conflict with the licence.** Contributors *"should ensure that the terms
   and conditions of the generative AI tool do not place any contractual restrictions on how the
   tool's output can be used that are inconsistent with the project's open source software license,
   the project's intellectual property policies, or the Open Source Definition."*[^lf-generative-ai]

   This obligation is **on the contributor and about their tool's EULA** — an unusual thing to ask,
   and something a contributor cannot discharge by inspecting their patch. It has to be checked once
   per tool, before use.

2. **Third-party material must be cleared and attributed.** Where pre-existing copyrighted material
   *"(including pre-existing open source code)"* appears in the output, the contributor must confirm
   permission — *"such as the form of an open source license or public domain declaration that
   complies with the project's licensing policies"* — and *"should provide notice and attribution of
   such third party rights, along with information about the applicable license terms, with their
   contribution."*[^lf-generative-ai]

## Tool features are named as a mitigation

The policy points at *"a feature that suppresses responses that are similar to third party
materials"*, and at similarity-flagging that *"provides information about the licensing terms that
apply to such third party materials."*[^lf-generative-ai] It is the only document here that treats
a **product setting** as part of compliance — which makes tool configuration, not just contributor
behaviour, part of what a downstream project might want to require.

## No general disclosure requirement

Attribution is required for **third-party material**. There is no requirement to disclose that
content was AI-generated as such.[^lf-generative-ai] So the LF baseline sits at the permissive end
of the spectrum this bundle is mapping: more permissive than [Ansible](../projects/ansible.md)'s
threshold-based SHOULD, and far more so than [Rust](../projects/rust.md)'s draft.

## It is a floor, not a ceiling

*"Individual Linux Foundation projects may develop their own project-specific
guidance."*[^lf-generative-ai] The Linux kernel's in-tree policy is exactly that, and it is
stricter. **Checking only this page and concluding "permitted" would be wrong for any project that
has written its own rule** — which, on this bundle's evidence, the more active ones have.

## What a contributor must do

Read your AI tool's terms of service once, and satisfy yourself it asserts nothing over the output
that conflicts with the project's licence. Turn on similarity suppression/flagging if your tool has
it. If third-party material surfaces in output you contribute, clear it and attribute it. Then check
whether the specific project has its own, stricter policy — because this page does not answer that.
