**Stance: restricted — and the restriction is not what a "ban" summary implies.** Git's
`SubmittingPatches` carries a section *"Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)"* whose operative
sentence is:[^git-submitting-patches-src]

> To avoid these issues, we will reject anything that looks AI generated, that sounds overly formal
> or bloated, that looks like AI slop, that looks good on the surface but makes no sense, or that
> senders don't understand or cannot explain.

**And, four lines later, in the same section:**

> We strongly recommend using AI tools carefully and responsibly.

Both sentences are in the document. A survey that records Git as a "complete ban" has reported the
first and dropped the second, and the two together are a materially different policy.

## What is actually prohibited

Read the rejection criteria as a list and none of them is *"you used a tool"*. They are properties
of the submission: it **looks** AI generated; it sounds overly formal or bloated; it is AI slop; it
looks good on the surface but makes no sense; the sender does not understand it or cannot explain
it. The last is the load-bearing one — it is the test a maintainer can actually apply, and it is
failable without any AI involvement at all.

This makes Git's rule an **output-and-comprehension standard**, where [QEMU](qemu.md)'s is an
input standard (*refrain from using*) and the [Linux kernel](linux-kernel.md)'s is a process
standard (*sign off, tag, take responsibility*). Three live shapes for the same problem.

## The reasoning: DCO, plus hallucination

The DCO *"requires contributors to certify that they know the origin of their contributions … It's
not yet clear that this can be legally satisfied when submitting significant amount of content that
has been generated by AI tools."* Separately, *"AIs still often hallucinate or just produce bad
code, commit messages, documentation or output, even when you point out their
mistakes."*[^git-submitting-patches-src]

Note the qualifier **"significant amount of"** — the copyright concern is scoped to volume, not
triggered by any assistance whatsoever.

## The recommended use is spelled out

The section closes by describing use it endorses: contributors *"would often benefit more from AI by
using it to guide and help them step by step towards producing a solution by themselves rather than
by asking for a full solution that they would then mostly copy-paste."* Also endorsed — debugging,
and *"checking for obvious mistakes, things that can be improved, things that don't match our style,
guidelines or our feedback, before sending it to us."*[^git-submitting-patches-src]

A project looking for prior art on "assistive yes, generative no" has it here, written by the
project that ships the DCO tooling everyone else uses.

## What a contributor must do

Be able to explain every line you send, in your own words, under review. Do not submit bulk
generated content. Strip the register — "overly formal or bloated" is named as a rejection trigger,
so prose that reads as machine-written will be treated as machine-written regardless of origin.
