**Stance: permitted, with responsibility as the whole mechanism.** CPython's developer guide carries
guidance on AI tools whose operative sentence is:

> The person submitting an issue or PR is responsible for its content, regardless of whether AI
> tools were used in its creation.[^python-devguide-ai-tools]

The survey being replaced filed Python under *"developing guidelines"*. It has published ones, and
they have been revised at least once.

## Disclosure is appreciated, not required

This is the distinguishing choice. Disclosure *"is appreciated, while not required"* — but
contributors *"must be prepared to explain the tool's usage and modifications"* and should be able
to explain their proposed changes independently.[^python-devguide-ai-tools]

Compare the alternatives now visible across this bundle: [Ansible](ansible.md) says disclosure
SHOULD happen above a significance threshold; the [Linux kernel](linux-kernel.md) requires a
structured tag; [Rust](rust.md)'s draft makes it mandatory for a whole tier and treats lying as a
Code of Conduct violation. Python declines all three and keeps only the **explain-it test** — which
is the same test [Git](git.md) reaches by a different route.

## What is explicitly acceptable

*"Assistance with writing comments, especially in a non-native language"*, *"Gaining understanding
of existing code"*, and *"Supplementing contributor knowledge for code, tests, and
documentation."*[^python-devguide-ai-tools]

The non-native-language allowance recurs — [FreeBSD](../distributions/freebsd.md) names translation
as its clearest AI use, and Rust's draft grants machine translation conditional status. A policy
that forbids generated prose outright taxes non-native speakers, and the projects that have thought
about it say so.

## The one hard prohibition

> It is not acceptable to alter or bypass existing tests, or remove desired functionality, in order
> to make a failing test pass.[^python-devguide-ai-tools]

Note that this is not an AI rule at all — it is a rule about a **failure mode AI makes cheap**. An
agent optimising for a green test suite will do exactly this, and the prohibition names the outcome
rather than the tool, so it holds however the change was produced.

## Enforcement is social and blunt

Maintainers *"may close issues and PRs that are not useful or productive, including those that are
fully generated by AI"*, without explanation; a contributor who *"repeatedly opens unproductive
issues or PRs"* may be blocked.[^python-devguide-ai-tools] There is no appeal process described.

Ordinary quality expectations still apply regardless of tooling: consider whether the change is
necessary, keep it minimal and focused, follow existing style, write tests, preserve backward
compatibility.[^python-devguide-ai-tools]

## What a contributor must do

Review it. Be able to explain it without the tool in front of you. Do not make a failing test pass
by changing the test. Disclosure is optional — but volunteering it costs nothing and pre-empts the
question you must be able to answer anyway.
